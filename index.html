ⓘ How to Perform the Swim Test

This calculator requires times from a 100/500 or 200/800 swim test performed as a single back-to-back effort with 1 minute of recovery between the two distances.

  • Short distance (100 or 200): Swim as fast as possible — this is an all-out sprint effort from a push start.
  • Rest 1 minute, then immediately begin the longer distance.
  • Long distance (500 or 800): Swim at the fastest pace you can sustain for the full distance — hard but controlled.

Accurate test times are essential for reliable pace zones. Long-course athletes (70.3 / Ironman) are encouraged to use the 200/800 test for better race-specific data, while short-course athletes may prefer the 100/500.

Count your strokes (optional): During each test swim, count your strokes on every length. Count each time a hand enters the water. Add them up and divide by the number of lengths to get your average strokes per length (SPL) for the short and the long swim. Enter both to get stroke count feedback on top of your pace zones, or leave both blank to skip it. Count per 25 yard or 25 meter length. In a 50 m pool, use half of your count per length.

New to swimming? If you know your short distance time but haven't yet completed the full test, use the ⚡ Estimate button next to the longer distance field. It will calculate an approximate time based on your short distance multiplied out and adding 25%. This is a starting point — completing the full test will always give you more accurate pace zones.

📖 Workout Shorthand Reference
Pace Zones
V2Recovery — Easy aerobic pace; used for warm-up, cool-down, and active recovery sets
V3Endurance — Steady aerobic pace; the foundation of distance training
V4Threshold — Comfortably hard; at or near lactate threshold intensity
V5VO2max — High intensity; builds maximal oxygen uptake capacity
V6Speed — Maximal sprint efforts; develops neuromuscular power
Effort-Based Paces
VEEasy effort — used for non-freestyle sets, drills, or kick work
VMModerate effort — steady, controlled exertion
VHHard effort — near-maximum perceived exertion
Rest & Recovery
RPSRest Per Set — seconds of rest between each rep within a set (e.g. between each 50 in 8×50)
RBBRest Between Blocks — rest between blocks within a set (e.g. after each group of 8×50 in 3×8×50)
RASRest After Set — total rest taken after the entire set before moving to the next one
Stroke Types
FREEFreestyle / Front Crawl (default if no stroke listed)
BACKBackstroke
BRSTBreaststroke
FLYButterfly
KICKKick only (no arms)
PULLPull — arms only, no paddles
PULL/PDSPull with paddles
Example: How to Read a Workout 4 × 150 @V3, 15RPS, 30RAS
→ Swim 4 repetitions of 150 at your V3 (Endurance) pace.
→ Take 15 seconds rest between each of the 4 reps.
→ Take 30 seconds rest after completing all 4 before moving on.

4 (2 × 25) kick @VM, 20RPS, 30RBB
→ 4 blocks of 2×25 kick at moderate effort.
→ 20 seconds rest between each 25 within a block.
→ 30 seconds rest between each block of 2×25.
